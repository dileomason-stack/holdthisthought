Privacy Policy

Hold This Thought

Last updated: October 31, 2026

Hold This Thought is built so your thoughts stay yours. There are no accounts, no ads, and no tracking, and we never sell or share data. The developer never sees your places, contacts, or location. If “Help improve the app” is on, the words of your thoughts, with names and numbers removed and nothing that identifies you, are sent to help the app understand thoughts better (see Helping improve the app). Otherwise the only things we receive are emails you choose to send us. Hold This Thought is made by Mason Di Leo, an independent developer.

Your thoughts

Your thoughts, reminders, photos you attach, saved places, and settings are stored on your device. If iCloud backup is on (Settings → Backup), a copy is also saved to your own private iCloud account so you can restore it on a new iPhone. That copy is in Apple's CloudKit private database: only you can access it, not the developer. You can turn backup off anytime. Photos are saved without the hidden location data cameras add. A thought due soon, or a store list while you shop, can show on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; that is drawn on your iPhone, like a notification.

Helping improve the app

To get better at telling whether a thought is about a time, a place, a grocery, or just a note, the app learns from real thoughts. When the setting Help improve the app is on, for each thought you save or change the app sends: its words, what the app made it (Time, Place, Grocery, or Thought), what it would have guessed from the words alone, the kind of place if it's a store or a common place like “Home” (otherwise just “custom”), and the app version.

Before anything is sent, on your iPhone: names, email addresses, phone numbers, long numbers, links, and street addresses are replaced with placeholders like [name], and thoughts that mention passwords, PINs, cards, or accounts aren't sent at all. Thoughts longer than 280 characters and thoughts friends share with you are never sent. Photos, your location, where your saved places are, your calendar, and your contacts are never sent.

Not linked to you: no name, account, iCloud ID, device ID, or advertising ID is sent, so we can't tell who a thought came from. Each thought gets a random code only so that a later change to it can be matched to it. The data is stored in a Google Sheet that only the developer can open (Google processes it under Google's Privacy Policy; like any web service it sees the connection's IP address, which we don't store). It is used only to train and test the model that reads thoughts in this app, is never sold or used for ads, and is deleted when it's no longer needed for that, at most three years after it's received.

Your choice: in the United States the setting starts on, and the app explains it the first time you open it. Everywhere else it starts off and nothing is sent unless you turn it on. You can turn it off anytime in Settings → Help improve the app; thoughts not yet sent are then deleted from your iPhone. Because sent thoughts can't be traced back to you, we can't find and delete yours individually afterwards.

Friends and shared thoughts

When you add a friend, you pick them from your contacts. Their name and phone number or email are used on your iPhone to address the invitation and to find their Apple Account through Apple's iCloud, so only they can open it. The invitation is sent as a text from your Messages app. The developer never receives your contacts.

When you share a thought with a friend, its words, time, and place (if it has one) are saved in a space in your iCloud that only you and that friend can open, and a copy appears in their app. They see when you check it off, and you see when they do. Your first name, as set in the app, is shown to them. Removing a friend ends the sharing; thoughts already shared stay on their phone as their own. Blocking someone also removes everything they shared from your app.

Groups (iCloud)

If you create or join a group, that group's tasks, member names, and optional places are stored in the group creator's iCloud using Apple's CloudKit and shared only with people they invite. The developer cannot access them. Leaving a group removes it from your iCloud; deleting a group you created removes it for everyone.

If a group task has a place, the first member to arrive claims it: the group sees who claimed it and when, so other members aren’t reminded too.

Location

Location is used only to deliver reminders you tie to a place, like "when I get home," "next time I'm at Trader Joe's," or "when I leave Grace's." Whether you've arrived or left is checked on your device. To find a place you haven't saved, the app searches Apple Maps, which receives the search and your approximate area under Apple's Privacy Policy. Your location is never sent to the developer. A place you attach to a shared thought is visible to the friend you share it with.

Microphone and speech

When you tap the mic, your words are turned into text using Apple's speech recognition. On iOS 26 and later this happens on your iPhone with Apple's on-device speech engine, so the audio doesn't leave your device (the first time, iPhone downloads the language files for it). On earlier versions, or until that download finishes, Apple may process the audio under Apple's Privacy Policy, depending on your device and settings. The app does not store recordings.

Calendar, camera, and photos

Calendar events are read on your device to time reminders around your schedule (for example, "before the party"), and events are only added when you ask. Photos you take or pick are stored on your device with the thought.

Apple Intelligence

On supported iPhones, short titles for your thoughts are written by Apple's on-device model. Your words don't leave the device for this.

Apple's role

The app relies on Apple's services (iCloud/CloudKit, Apple Maps, speech recognition, and notifications). Apple handles that data under its own Privacy Policy. No other companies are involved, except Google: emails you send us are stored with our email provider (Google), and, if Help improve the app is on, the anonymous thoughts described above are stored in Google Sheets.

When you contact us or report someone

If you email us, or tap Report or Contact in the app (which opens an email for you to send), we receive what's in that email: your email address, your message, and, for a report, the person's name and the text of what they shared. It is used only to answer you and to deal with the report, isn't shared with anyone, and is deleted within 90 days after the matter is closed. You can ask us to delete it sooner.

Deleting your data

To keep a copy, Settings → Export thoughts makes a text file that you choose where to save or send. Delete a thought by swiping it, or delete the app to remove everything on your iPhone. To delete the iCloud copy, turn off backup in the app (Settings → Backup) and tap Delete iCloud backup. You can also open iPhone Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Hold This Thought → Delete Data. The developer never has your iCloud copy, so there is nothing for the developer to delete there; to delete an email you sent us, just ask. For anonymous thoughts sent to help improve the app, see Helping improve the app.

Do Not Track

The app doesn't track you across apps or websites, so there is nothing for a Do Not Track signal to turn off.

Children

Hold This Thought isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect personal information from them. Apart from emails you choose to send, the developer doesn't collect personal information from anyone; thoughts sent to help improve the app are anonymous, with names and numbers removed.

Your rights (including EU and UK)

You control your data directly on your device and in your iCloud, and can view, change, or delete it at any time as described above. If you have a question or request about your data, email the address below. If you’re in the EU or UK, you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date, and significant changes will be mentioned in the app's update notes.
